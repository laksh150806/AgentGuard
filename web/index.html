YOUR PROJECT, IN YOUR WORDS
A clear interview story.
“I’m studying how application permissions can limit harmful tool actions when an AI agent reads untrusted content.”


Untrusted document↓Agent proposes an action↓Trusted validation + authorization↓Tool result + audit event


What have we built?
Three synthetic support tools, ownership and refund checks, retry protection, a bounded local-model loop, paired evaluations, and a pinned AgentDojo adapter. The model does not choose the user's identity or permission policy.


Why not just tell the model to ignore attacks?
A prompt asks the model to behave. Enforcement checks the proposed tool action before execution. Compare both under the same tasks and attacks to measure their effects.


What does this not solve?
A wrong ₹6 refund can fit within a ₹20 limit. Authorization alone cannot prove user intent. This project also has no broad benchmark success or novel defense claim yet.


What should I say about the results?
The model passed three simple support tasks. One attack failed against every mode, so it showed no defense advantage. The banking benchmark remains unreliable, even on clean tasks. Those failures are preserved.


What is next?
Establish reliable clean-task performance with a free model suitable for available hardware. Then repeat matched attack comparisons, expand held-out cases, and measure lost utility, latency and cost.


Try saying it aloud. Explain why a document cannot grant permission, why zero successful attacks can be misleading, and why a stricter policy can be worse for legitimate users.
